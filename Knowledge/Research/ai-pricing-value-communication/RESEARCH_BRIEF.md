## monday.com today vs. in-progress direction

**Live pricing (Work Management, fetched 2026-03-29):** AI is mostly listed as **“AI credits”** plus **“AI Sidekick (lite)”** / **“(plus)”** on Enterprise. Enterprise adds an **“One AI”** narrative block (custom software, context-aware assistant, “run AI powered features”) — story-first, light on interpretable limits. Screenshot: `monday.com_pricing.png`.

**In-progress mockup:** Headline shifts to **platform + depth of AI** (“The best AI work platform”). Value is expressed as **bundled quotas** (credits/month, agent tasks, workflows, Vibe app count, notetaking hours, Sidekick tier). This improves **comparability across tiers** but still leads with **activity metrics**, not end-user outcomes (unless each metric is defined in plain language).

## Visual / copy patterns (what the market is doing)

1. **Outcome-first bullets** — Notion-style: each AI line answers “what work goes away / gets faster?”  
2. **Unlimited + fair-use + top-up credits** — ClickUp-style: simple mental model for core assistant, meter for heavy agents/automation.  
3. **Separate AI strip** — ClickUp embeds a full AI comparison *below* core plans so buyers don’t mix “seats” with “AI depth” mentally.  
4. **Plan-gated depth** — Slack / Notion: higher tier = richer AI (search scope, workflow generation, etc.), not always more tokens on the page.  
5. **Credits as plumbing, not headline** — Asana / Atlassian / Notion Custom Agents: credits appear when metering is honest; pairing with **examples** (“~N automations” or “~M searches”) reduces abstraction.  
6. **Enterprise = bundle + governance** — Microsoft / Salesforce: value = deployment surface + control + procurement fit, not feature bullets alone.

---

## Key trends

- **Hybrid packaging** is common: subscription seat + **usage envelope** for agents/heavy AI.  
- **“Unlimited”** sells well for assistants; **metered credits** show up for agents, images, or cross-tool automation.  
- **Outcome copy** is increasingly used *alongside* limits, not instead of them — limits build trust; outcomes drive comprehension.  
- **Separate AI section** reduces confusion between “collaboration features” and “automation capacity.”

---

## Implications for monday.com (value-based, outcome-clear)

1. **Translate each mockup metric** into one short outcome clause, e.g. notetaking hours → “Auto-notes and action items for up to ~X meeting hours/month” (adjust copy to legal/comms standards). Same for “agent tasks” → “delegated actions completed for you (updates, drafts, board changes).”  
2. **Keep credits as the unified meter** but add a **“What credits buy”** link or tooltip with 2–3 concrete examples per plan.  
3. **Tier story = persona + job**, not only numbers — your mockup already has one-line descriptions; align bullets so each column answers: *What will my team stop doing manually?*  
4. **Consider a split layout** like ClickUp: core work management comparison + **AI bundle comparison** strip so Sidekick / Vibe / Agents aren’t flattened into one undifferentiated list.  
5. **Challenge assumption:** Identical Business vs Pro AI quotas (as in the mockup) undermine upgrade logic — either differentiate enterprise value (governance, support, custom) or adjust AI envelopes.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Usability test** the mockup with 5–8 buyers: “In your own words, what do you get?” — flag any metric that fails without explanation.  
2. **Publish a lightweight glossary** (one page) for “credit,” “AI workflow,” “agent task,” and “Vibe app” — linked from pricing.  
3. **A/B copy** on the hero: platform-first vs outcome-first (“Ship work faster with AI across boards, docs, and customer workflows”) and measure scroll-to-compare and CTA clicks.
