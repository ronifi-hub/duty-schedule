Trial Patterns

6 Patterns We Found

Every competitor's approach maps to one of these patterns. Pattern 6 is the emerging dominant strategy.

1. Usage-Capped Free Tier

Salesforce · Base44 · Airtable · HubSpot · ClickUp

Free forever with hard credit cap. Zero friction, but cap may be too low to reach aha moment. Salesforce: ~10K free actions. Base44: ~33/month.

2. Time-Boxed Free Trial

Intercom (14 days) · Microsoft

Full access for fixed window. Creates urgency but may be too short for complex setups. Intercom: no CC required.

3. Outcome-Based Pricing

Zendesk · Intercom

Pay only when AI resolves issues. Aligns cost with value. No work management platform does this yet — potential differentiator.

4. Tier-Gated Access

HubSpot · Asana · ClickUp (Super Agents)

Agents locked behind higher tiers. Drives upsell but high friction — users can't try agents without $500+/mo commitment.

5. Developer Sandbox

Salesforce

Free isolated environment. Good for technical buyers, doesn't prove business value. Every Dev org includes Agentforce by default.

6. Launch Free, Charge Later ⭐

Notion (10 weeks) · HubSpot (grace period)

Ship free, announce billing date, charge later. Maximizes adoption. Users build dependency, then convert. Emerging dominant strategy in work management.

Market Signals

6 Key Trends

What the competitive landscape tells us about where the market is heading.

1. Per-seat → usage pricing

Salesforce, Zendesk, Intercom align cost with value delivered, not headcount.

2. Credits are universal

Every work management competitor uses credits. The term is now standard across the industry.

3. Self-serve is table stakes

ServiceNow is the only outlier with zero self-serve — enterprise-only by design.

4. Grace periods = new trial

Notion (free 10 weeks) and HubSpot launch free, charge later. Dominant in work management.

5. AI bundled into plans

Airtable, ClickUp, Notion embed credits in tiers — agents aren't a separate product to buy.

6. Aha before paywall

Best trials guarantee users experience real agent value before asking for money.

So What

Implications for monday.com

Opportunities and risks based on what competitors are doing with trial mechanisms.

Opportunity

Notion's grace period is the #1 playbook

Same category, same buyer, launched 3 weeks ago. Free 10 weeks, then credits. Study their communication, dashboard UX, and free→paid transition.

Opportunity

Bundled credits = emerging standard

Airtable, ClickUp, Notion embed AI credits in existing tiers. Doing the same would feel natural and avoid friction of a separate agent purchase.

Opportunity

Outcome-based pricing is unclaimed

No work management platform charges per "outcome." If monday can define a clear outcome unit, this is a real differentiator.

Risk

Notion sets the bar at 10 weeks free

Anything less generous from monday.com will feel stingy by comparison. The expectation is now set.

Risk

Stacking agent cost on seat cost

ClickUp's $22/user/mo on top of base pricing adds up fast for large teams. Total cost becomes a barrier.

Risk

Billing cliff kills conversion

Abrupt cutoff when credits expire is the worst UX. Notion pauses gracefully — monday needs an equivalent.

Insights

What They Get Right — and Wrong

Experience patterns worth adopting and antipatterns to avoid.

What works

Agent builds itself from intent

Notion: describe what you want, agent generates its own config, tools, and triggers. Users don't configure — they describe. Lowest friction, most magical.

What works

Value visible immediately

Airtable shows outputs inline in table cells. You don't go somewhere else to check results. The value is right where you're already working.

What works

Test before deploy

Intercom's preview panels let you simulate conversations before going live. For agents that modify real data, a "dry run" mode gives confidence and reduces fear.

What works

Usage transparency from day one

Notion shows a credit dashboard during the free period — users see what agents cost before paying. Removes "surprise bill" fear and builds trust.

What fails

Prerequisites kill momentum

Salesforce requires enabling Einstein and Data Cloud before you can open Agent Builder. Every prerequisite step is a dropout point in the trial funnel.

What fails

No guidance on what to build first

Most platforms drop users into a blank agent builder. Base44's starter prompts and Intercom's templates are rare exceptions. Users need a suggested first use case.

What fails

Background agents delay the aha moment

Notion agents run async — set them up and wait. For a trial, this means users have to check back later. The "it works!" moment is delayed, not instant.

What fails

Hard cutoff when limits hit

Base44 agents just go silent when credits run out. For users who built dependency during the trial, this feels like punishment, not a nudge to upgrade.

Recommendations

UX Principles for monday.com Agents Trial

Six design principles derived from the best and worst competitor experiences.

1

Let users describe, not configure

Follow Notion — conversational agent creation where the user states intent and the agent generates its own setup. No field-by-field configuration.

Inspired by: Notion's "write a job description" setup
2

Show value inline, not in a dashboard

Follow Airtable — agent outputs should appear where users already work (boards, items, columns), not in a separate agent management screen.

Inspired by: Airtable's in-cell agent fields
3

Include a "try it now" dry run

Follow Intercom — let users preview what an agent would do before it runs for real. Especially important for agents that modify board data or send notifications.

Inspired by: Intercom's preview panels in the Fin Flywheel
4

Suggest the first agent, don't show a blank builder

Based on the user's board structure, most common workflows, or most repetitive tasks — recommend a specific first agent. Don't make them imagine the use case.

Inspired by: Base44's starter prompts; gap identified across most competitors
5

Never interrupt active work

Follow Notion's degradation model — agents finish current runs and pause at the billing boundary. Show warnings well before limits are hit. No mid-action cutoffs.

Inspired by: Notion's "pause at next service date" model
6

Make usage visible from day one

Show a usage/credit dashboard during the free period so users can forecast costs and feel in control. Transparency builds trust and reduces upgrade anxiety.

Inspired by: Notion's credit dashboard during free beta
